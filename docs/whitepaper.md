# AttestFlow: Cross-Chain Verified Payment Engine

**BUIDL CTC 2026 Fall — Track: AI**
*An autonomous AI agent that turns real cross-chain payments into cryptographically verified, automatically settled outcomes — powered by the Attestcoin Protocol.*

---

## 1. Problem

Cross-chain "payment acceptance" today relies on either centralized bridges (custody risk, opaque off-chain operators) or oracle networks that deliver *data about* payments but rarely carry *guarantees about* them. For a freelancer waiting on an invoice, or a business reconciling cross-border receivables, three things are missing:

1. **Trustless proof** that a specific payment really happened and succeeded on the source chain.
2. **Autonomous execution** the moment that proof exists — no human reconciling invoices.
3. **A closed loop**: the outcome must travel back to the other chain as a verified message, not a screenshot.

Meanwhile, LLM-based agents excel at interpreting intent ("watch my incoming invoices, release funds at 10%") but hallucinate when asked to trust unverified chain data.

## 2. Solution

AttestFlow is an agent-native payment engine that separates **intent** (natural language), **truth** (Attestcoin Protocol cryptographic proofs), and **execution** (a deterministic smart-contract state machine):

- **Intent layer** — a natural-language rule ("当我在 Sepolia 收到 ≥100 USDC 时，按 10% 释放") is compiled into a structured policy by a deterministic parser (LLM optional, disclosed). Determinism here is a feature: money rules must be reproducible.
- **Truth layer** — the Attestcoin Protocol's readability proves, *on Creditcoin*, that a specific Sepolia transaction is included in a confirmed source-chain block. The proof is generated by the official hosted prover and verified by the native `0x0FD2` precompile at EVM speed.
- **Execution layer** — `AttestFlowASC` (an Attestcoin Smart Contract) re-checks what the protocol does not cover — receipt success, replay, policy match — then releases escrowed CTC and emits a writability message. A relayer delivers it to Sepolia, where an validating inbox executes it.

The agent is the *muscle* (watching, proving, relaying), the ASC is the *spine* (trust), and the protocol is the *nervous system* connecting two chains.

## 4. Attestcoin Protocol integration depth

| Protocol surface | Usage |
|---|---|
| ChainInfo precompile | Runtime discovery of supported source chains (`chainKey`), attested heights |
| Proof generation | Official hosted prover (`ProofBuilder`), Merkle + continuity proofs |
| BlockProver `0x0FD2` | Synchronous in-ASC verification (`verify()`) of every settled payment |
| Encoding v1 | On-chain decoding of attested transaction bytes, incl. receipt status |
| Writability semantics | Four-step publish→sign→deliver→validate loop (adapter until official Outbox/Inbox ship) |

**Engineering findings contributed back** (detailed in `docs/integration.md`):

- Contract-context `verifyAndEmit()` reverts on CC3 testnet; `verify()` is the correct in-ASC primitive today.
- Real Sepolia USDC traffic is dominated by `transferFrom` (selector `0x23b872dd`); policies must match calldata, not just `Transfer` events.
- Scanning the *attested window* (prover REST) eliminates attestation-latency stalls while remaining fully proof-backed.

## 5. Security model

1. **Cryptographic**: every settlement requires a valid Merkle + continuity proof against attestor-committed checkpoints. No agent decision can mint a settlement without it.
2. **Beyond inclusion**: the protocol proves inclusion, not success. The ASC decodes the attested receipt and rejects `status != 1` — a dedicated test covers this adversarial path.
3. **Replay**: `keccak(chainKey, height, txIndex)` is single-use on-chain; the agent additionally dedupes per tx hash across restarts.
4. **Least trust in the agent**: the relayer/operator cannot forge settlements (needs a valid proof), cannot double-settle (on-chain guard), cannot redirect funds (beneficiary is policy-fixed). Its only power is *when* to submit.
5. **Deterministic money rules**: policy compilation is deterministic by default; optional LLM parsing is disclosed and never used for settlement decisions.

## 6. Results (public testnets, reproducible)

- 6+ autonomous on-chain settlements driven by real third-party USDC payments.
- Example loop: source payment `0x39091951…` → settlement `0x9c8caf6b…` (CC3, 190–195k gas) → verified delivery `0xd6abf721…` (Sepolia).
- 14/14 contract tests including the security-critical rejection paths.
- End-to-end agent run (NL rule → settle → deliver) completes unattended in ~40 s once a matching payment exists in the attested window.

## 7. Roadmap

1. **Official writability swap** — replace the relayer adapter with Outbox/Inbox when they deploy on testnet (payload shape already aligned).
2. **Multi-source policies** — same engine, more chainKeys via ChainInfo registry.
3. **Quorum inbox** — attestor-set signature verification replacing the single authorized relayer.
4. **Invoice NFT receipts** — settlement mints a transferable proof-of-payment asset.
5. **LLM risk copilot** — anomaly commentary over settlement history (display-only; never on the money path).

## 8. Conclusion

AttestFlow demonstrates that the Attestcoin Protocol is sufficient — today, on public testnets — to power *autonomous, proof-gated cross-chain payments* with an AI agent in the driver's seat and cryptography in the guardrails. Every claim in this paper is backed by a transaction hash a judge can click.
